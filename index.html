Innate nucleic acid sensing

How a cell reacts to nucleic acids.

Every cell carries DNA and RNA that the immune system can read as danger. What follows that reading is our subject: a sustained interferon response, inflammation, a cell committed to a different fate, a genome full of mobile elements held quiet or let loose. We work on both ends of it — type I interferon–driven inflammation and the fundamental biology of retroelement defense — in primary human cells and in mouse models, and we carry both toward therapeutics.

Research

Two arms, one question
Arm 01 — Immunopathology

Type I interferon–driven inflammation

A persistent interferon response reshapes the cells that live through it, and it does so through hundreds of interferon-stimulated genes whose individual functions are largely unknown. We work through that space gene by gene — in primary human cells and in mouse models — to understand how the response turns into immunopathology, and to identify the nodes within it that can be drugged.

  • Interferon signature
  • Autoinflammation
  • Cell fate
  • Mouse models

Arm 02 — Fundamental biology

Retroelement defense

LINE-1 and related retroelements make up a large fraction of the human genome and never stop being transcribed entirely. We study how cells hold that activity in check, what escapes, and how the products are recognised by the nucleic acid sensing machinery.

  • LINE-1
  • cGAS–STING
  • Transposon control
  • Genome stability

The shared denominator is sensing. Both arms ask the same question from opposite ends — one from what a sustained response leaves behind, the other from the nucleic acid that sets it off. Methods, mouse lines and people move between them.

Across both arms

Therapeutics

Informed by our two research arms, we actively develop immune therapeutics. We identify novel targets and compounds, and test their efficacy in tailored cell culture models, in vivo models and small investigator-initiated clinical trials.

This work runs with academic groups and with industry partners, for whom we have been reliable collaborators since 2018.

  • Target identification
  • Compound screening
  • Cell culture models
  • In vivo models
  • Investigator-initiated trials
